One record, three people

A concern starts at home, is screened by an ASHA worker, and is seen at a primary health centre. Each person does their part. The record used to break in the gaps between them.

She sees who needs her first

The ASHA’s home screen is a work queue, not a dashboard. One row per person, with the next action in words and the IMNCI colour always beside a label, ranked by what is due.

Booked offline, sent when the signal returns

Saving never waits for signal. The visit sits on her phone as “1 visit to send”, and appears on the doctor’s list the moment it syncs. Offline is a calm state, not an error.

Right where each person is

A smartphone for the ASHA, any phone for the family, a desktop at the PHC. All three read and write the same record.

A matching Kannada voice flow is what I would build next.

ASHA app

Which family, who is unwell, one question at a time, then the result with what was found and what was ruled out.

What this is, and isn’t

A working prototype, not a production healthcare system. All three surfaces share one record in the browser. Clinical rules are illustrative, and every name, family and number is invented. Production would need clinical validation, secure identity, real telephony and persistent offline storage.
