Case study · Rupeezy

The switch that could only hold two

I was asked for a switch between our trading app and our mutual fund app. I shipped it on the deadline. Placing it is what showed me the model that had to replace it.

Role

Sole designer

Team

1 PM, 7 engineers

Timeline

Nov 2024 – Aug 2025

Live today

Stocks, F&O, commodities

01

Two apps. One customer. Neither one could show you everything you owned.

Rupeezy Trade held stocks and F&O. Rupeezy Invest, then called Investeezy, held mutual funds. To see how you were doing, you opened both apps and added the two totals up yourself.

And the trading app carried an advert for the other one. We were using our own product to send our own customers away to a second download.

02

Then a rule took most of the trading revenue.

Selling mutual funds had been the plan for years. It was never urgent enough to start. In October 2024 the regulator set a date, and it stopped being optional.

1 October 2024

SEBI announces new rules for index derivatives. Bigger contracts, one weekly expiry per exchange, more margin on expiry day. The aim is to push small retail traders out of F&O.

20 November 2024

First set of rules goes live. The smallest contract you can buy rises to ₹15 lakh.

15 January 2025

Phase one ships.

1 February 2025

Second set goes live. Option premium now has to be paid upfront.

March 2025

Trading revenue is down about 75%.

I pulled this in Firebase myself. March 2025 against September and October 2024, the months before the rules. Percentages only. It is the one number on this page I measured rather than was told.

Zerodha's founder had estimated the rules would hit about 60% of all F&O trades. We sat further out than that, because almost everything we earned came from one product.

97%

Of revenue, one product line

That is not concentration. That is exposure.

0

Cross-sell, either app

A fund investor could not buy a stock. A trader could not start an SIP. Anything we built next reached only the people who had that one app.

2×

Work, every release

Two developers per app, four of the seven engineers, shipping every rule change and every campaign twice.

Mutual funds were no longer extra income. They were the only other business we had.

03

Every other broker was already on one app.

I went through how the others handled more than one product. Groww, Angel One, Upstox and Paytm Money all sold stocks, F&O and mutual funds inside a single app. Angel One had folded funds into its trading app back in 2023.

Our own customers had noticed. This is from the Play Store reviews:

“Every other broker has one app, so why do I need two?”

From the reviews

So the question was never whether to merge. It was how fast, and what to build first.

04

I never spoke to a customer. Three things stood in for one.

Everything I knew about how people moved between the two products was second hand. I read it. I did not ask for it.

  • 1

    Play Store reviews. People were already asking why they needed two apps.

  • 2

    In-app feedback. What they told us without being asked.

  • 3

    Firebase. How people actually moved between the two products.

05

The answer was a switch, and it had six weeks.

Phase one would put Invest inside Trade, with a switch between them. It is the pattern food delivery apps use to move between food and groceries. Phase two would make it better once it was live.

I did not argue with that. Revenue was falling every week. A switch was the fastest thing that worked, and shipping something beats perfecting nothing.

For the business

Sell to both halves of our own customer base. Build every rule change and every campaign once instead of twice.

For the customer

One place where all the money lives. One number that says how you are doing, without opening a second app.

06

Where the switch lived was my call. I tried three places.

Tried, rejected

Top navigation. The top bar already carried indices, search and alerts. Putting the switch there meant redesigning screens we were not otherwise touching.

Tried, rejected

A floating button on Home. It can only live on one screen. Somebody three levels deep inside a stock would have to come all the way back out to change product.

Chose

Bottom navigation. It is on every screen, so the switch is one tap away from wherever you already are. And it disturbed the least.

Phase one shipped on 15 January 2025. Six weeks after design started, and two weeks before the second set of rules took effect.

I was placing the switch in the bottom bar when I saw what it could not do.

A switch holds two things. Commodities were already being discussed.

07

Stocks and mutual funds are not two jobs. They are two ways of doing one.

You put money somewhere, and you watch what it does. So two things run through the app at the same time. What you own, and what you are doing with it.

Those are two directions, not two products. Give each one its own row, and the app can hold as many products as you like.

What you own

You are looking at

Stocks · Portfolio

What you are doing

Tap either row. The row you did not touch stays exactly where it was.

08

I built it instead of explaining it.

The difference between a switch and two rows is hard to hear and easy to see. So I stopped talking and made a working prototype, the same one you just used.

If I cannot convince people with words, the problem is usually the format, not the argument.

I showed it to the design team first. Then to a VP Product and a Director. Everyone agreed it was the right direction.

There was no room for it. The regulation had put work into every team's queue, the switch was live and doing its job, and this needed real engineering on top of that.

So it was agreed, and it was parked. Phase two, when there was room.

The words had to change too. Position is F&O language. You open a position and you close it. Somebody buying a fund holds it for years. Watchlist is a trader's habit, so it became Explore, which is what a first-timer is actually doing.

10

Home breaks the pattern, on purpose.

Every other screen carries the product row. Home does not, and that is the one screen where the merge has to prove itself.

The call

Whether Home follows the same product-row pattern as every other screen.

Considered

Give Home the same row. It would be consistent, and you would always know which product you were in.

Chose

No product row on Home. Everything visible at once. With a row, somebody who trades would only ever see stocks on Home, and the whole point of the merge is that they see funds too.

What it cost

One screen breaks the pattern, and Home becomes the only place where you cannot tell what you are looking at from the navigation alone.

So Home leads with one number for everything you hold, puts IPO and NFO side by side so neither product is the guest, and uses words a beginner recognises. Hybrid. ELSS. Start with ₹100.

11

A first-timer lands where they said they wanted to be.

The call

Which product a first-time user sees first.

Considered

Always open on Stocks. Simple, and right for most people.

Chose

Use what they already told us at sign-up. Somebody who said they came to invest opens on Mutual Funds. Stocks only when we do not know.

What it cost

The fallback still favours traders, so somebody who skipped that question starts in the wrong place.

12

We moved people over a month, not overnight.

Sign-up was already shared between the two apps, so accounts linked themselves. Nobody had to verify anything again or upload a document.

Stage one

You can close it

A message inside the old app telling people about the move, with a close button. One month.

Stage two

You cannot

The same message, no close button. The only thing left to do is download the new app.

Stage three

It is gone

The old app leaves the stores.

Switching everybody in one go would have been faster. In an app that holds your money, something that suddenly stops working reads as broken, not as an upgrade.

13

The model is live today. It holds a product I never designed for.

Two rows, across stocks, F&O and commodities. Commodities arrived after I left.

Mutual funds is in the app too, reached by a switch in the bottom bar rather than by the product row.

I left in August 2025, before any of this was built.

I opened the current app and checked.

What I did not do

No testing with customers. The decisions came from the business, the data and my own reasoning.

No measure agreed up front. We never said what this should move before we shipped it, so I cannot tell you whether cross-sell improved.

What I can claim

Three products on one model. Stocks, F&O and commodities.

Four developers to three. They had been split two per app.

Rule changes shipped once, not twice.
