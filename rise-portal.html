The first product Rupeezy’s partners ever had

A partner portal that shows the funnel and the payout calculation, not just the total.

The short version

Rupeezy had around 100 digital partners driving a third of its revenue, and the whole relationship ran on spreadsheets and phone calls. A partner who wanted to know how many leads had converted rang their relationship manager.

I designed Rise Portal, the first product those partners ever had. Four screens that answer the two questions every call was about: how many of my leads converted, and how much will I earn this cycle.

Lead-to-client conversion went up 2.4×. Support tickets dropped 48%. And partners started doing what the funnel was built for.

03·What was broken

Refer more, earn less, and no way to find out why

A partner wanting to know how their leads had converted called their relationship manager, who opened a spreadsheet and read the numbers back.

A hundred partners, referring daily, each entitled to ask at any time. The share varied by period, anywhere from 10% to 60%, and payouts landed on the 15th of every month.

“Last month I referred 100 people and earned X. This month I referred 200 and earned less. Why?”

The call the support team took most.

What the payout actually depended on

Leadscompleted KYC
Clientsactually traded
Volumehow much they traded
Ratethe share that month

The partner could see none of it — only the total, once a month, on the 15th.

When a third of your revenue flows through people who can’t verify their own income, you don’t have a reporting gap. You have a trust problem.

That’s the problem. What we wanted from fixing it is next.

04·What we were after

Two questions, answered before anyone had to ask

Every call came down to the same two things. The product exists to answer them.

Question one

“How many of my leads converted?”

Question two

“How much will I earn this cycle?”

For the business

Transparency, visibility and trust. The relationship was leaking on all three, and a third of revenue ran through it.

For the partner

See your own business. Where every referral is, what’s stuck, what each day earns, which is enough to decide where to put effort next.

Every screen answers one of those two questions, and the funnel was built so partners could act on what they saw.

Four screens, one job each.

05·How I went at it

Show the calculation, not just the total

If a partner can see how a number was made, they don’t need to call and ask.

Responsive web, both ends. The same four sections in a bottom navigation on mobile, with charts and tables restacked for one thumb.

Dashboard

Earnings three ways, current cycle, last payout and all-time, with a daily chart. Below it sits the funnel, showing total leads and then the three states a lead can be in.

The funnel wasn’t only reporting. A partner who sees a referral stuck at “account processing” knows that person. The design bet was that they’d pick up the phone, and that their call would land better than ours.

Nothing about the money is computed out of sight.

Two things made this harder than four screens.

06·What got in the way

A compound rule and a compliance line

One rule that couldn’t be simplified, and one piece of data that couldn’t be shown.

One: making “X and (Y or Z)” readable

Partners don’t get paid automatically every cycle. Ten active clients, mandatory, AND either 30% of total clients active OR five new clients that cycle. The rule came from the business. Partners needed to read their own status, not parse a logic expression.

“A message saying only ‘you’re not eligible for payout’ would produce exactly the phone call the whole product was built to remove.”

Kunal, Product Manager

The call
Where payout eligibility sits on the dashboard.
Considered
Top of the page. It decides whether a partner gets paid, so it was argued to be the most important thing there.
Chose
Bottom right. Eligibility is one-time information. You need it while working toward the threshold and stop looking once you’ve met it. Earnings and clients are checked every visit.
Cost
A partner who has never read the rule has to scroll to find it.

I also considered hiding the card once a partner qualified, and rejected it. A dashboard that rearranges itself by state is harder to learn than one stable layout.

Two: the number we couldn’t show

As a regulated broker, we can’t expose client phone numbers, not to partners and not to anyone. But most partners already collect contact details through their own forms. What they need from the portal isn’t the number. It’s the match.

The call
How much of a client’s identity to expose to the partner who referred them.
Considered
Nothing beyond the name. The safest reading of the rule.
Chose
Name, masked digits, status, last activity. The smallest set that lets a partner match against their own records.
Cost
A partner without their own records can’t reach a stuck lead through the portal.

The rule was legible and the line was held. Here’s the rest of the product.

07·What I built

Earnings, clients, and the list that became a call sheet

Three more screens, and a mobile version built for one hand.

Earnings

Earnings plotted against total clients and against traded clients, so a partner can see which clients actually generate revenue. Below it, payout history with bank account, cycle, amount, deductions and date.

The daily chart does more than show a total. A partner can see that Monday and Friday carry the volume, and put effort into the weaker days, where the same work returns more.

Clients

Every client with their last-traded date, split into active and inactive, not by signup date and not alphabetically.

Mobile

Important and frequent are not the same thing. Layout follows frequency.

It shipped. Then the bet came in.

08·What happened next

Partners started doing what the funnel was built for

Two months after launch, the business team confirmed it: partners were chasing their own stuck leads.

A partner sees a referral stuck at “account processing”. That lead is money one step from becoming a client, so they call — a call that used to be the KYC support team’s job.

The inactive list works the same way. An inactive client earns the partner nothing, so partners nudge them back. The product made their pipeline visible; the design bet was that self-interest would do the rest. It did.

Rise Portal turned 100 partners into a distributed sales layer that costs nothing and converts better than a cold call.

And a way to recruit

The portal became something the business team could show a prospective partner. Before, the pitch was a commission rate. After, it was a screen.

What I’d do differently
No direct contact

I never spoke to a partner. I designed for a behaviour I predicted and couldn’t observe. Five conversations before launch would have told me how close the bet was.

No plan for the numbers

We didn’t agree what this should move. The 2.4× and the 48% exist because I went and asked the business and support teams afterwards.

No evidence, only reasoning

I made the placement case on frequency alone. I was right, and I had nothing to show. A screen recording of five partners would have settled it in a minute.
