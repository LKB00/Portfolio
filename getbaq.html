Designing an AI agent that fights for your refund

Ananya has ₹3,499 stuck with Flipkart and a bot that keeps saying “5 to 7 working days”. The interface is a conversation. The product is a case-resolution system.

Project
Self-initiated AI product, Sep 2026
Role
Product designer, end to end, built with an AI coding assistant
Status
Working prototype. Eval and user tests not yet run

Getbaq turns a messy consumer dispute into a structured case: it understands the evidence, finds the rule that applies, prepares the next action, tracks what happens next, and keeps her in control of every consequential decision.

01 · The problem

A refund isn’t a writing problem. It’s a follow-through problem.

She may have a screenshot, a payment receipt, a chat with the bot and an order number, and still not know what to do next. The law says Flipkart must resolve her complaint within a month, and a free helpline can push it. She doesn’t know either exists.

66%

of people with a major service problem got no help from the company

LocalCircles survey, 2026

1,000+

companies answer complaints sent through 1915, India’s free National Consumer Helpline

National Consumer Helpline

What she has to do today

  1. Find the rule
  2. Calculate the deadline
  3. Write the complaint
  4. Remember to follow up
  5. Work out when to escalate

What Getbaq handles

  1. Understands the evidence
  2. Verifies the rule that applies
  3. Prepares the next action
  4. Tracks the case
  5. Prepares the escalation

ChatGPT can write the complaint. Getbaq is designed to run the case: not to generate a good response, but to move a real-world case toward resolution.

02 · Why an agent

She shouldn’t have to become the workflow.

A traditional refund flow asks her to structure the information, find the policy, count the days, write the complaint, send it and remember what to do next. An agent can take that work off her.

  1. Give me what you have
  2. I understand
  3. I investigate
  4. I verify
  5. I prepare
  6. You approve
  7. I monitor

The agent handles the work. Deterministic code handles what should never be guessed. She stays responsible for anything consequential.

03 · The design question

How do you make an agent useful without giving it too much control?

Most agents hand over more control as trust grows. I tied control to the consequence instead: the higher the stakes and the harder an action is to undo, the more control goes back to her.

Stakes ↑

Can be undone ← → Can’t be undone

Can be undone

Show it, and let her correct it

  • The facts it read
  • The rule it picked
  • The deadline
Can’t be undone

Always her tap

  • Sending the complaint
  • Escalating to 1915
Can be undone

Just do it, and show it

  • Reading her message or screenshot
  • Drafting the letter
Can’t be undone

Do it, and tell her

  • Stripping an OTP she pastedremoved before the model sees it, and she’s told

The agent can do the work, but it never becomes the person responsible.

04 · The boundary

AI reads. Code counts. She decides.

I split the system by what each part is best at. The model interprets messy language and screenshots. Code does dates, amounts and state, which can be calculated and shouldn’t be guessed. She owns the consequences.

05 · Decision 01

Don’t ask the model to calculate what can be calculated.

A language model can produce a convincing answer and still get a deadline wrong, and a confidently wrong deadline is worse than no agent: she’d wait for a date that has already passed. So calculation lives outside the model.

  1. AI finds the ruleIt reads her case and picks the one rule that applies.
  2. Code calculates the deadlineThe model is never given a day count, so it has nothing to miscalculate.
  3. The UI says CalculatedThe date is labelled with where it came from, not presented as the AI’s opinion.

The design move

It wasn’t about making the model smarter. It was about giving it less opportunity to be wrong.

06 · Decision 02

Don’t hide the work behind “High confidence”.

A confidence label on its own just asks her to trust the system. So every important claim carries a trail: where it came from, and how it was checked.

ClaimWhere it came fromHow it’s checked
₹3,499 is at stakeWhat she wroteShe confirms it
E-Commerce Rules, Rule 4(4) appliesThe rule sheet, read from the lawVerified
Flipkart must reply by 3 Sep 2026Her complaint date, 4 Aug, plus 30 daysCalculated
The letter says only what she confirmedThe facts she approvedSafety check
  1. Every step says who did itAI, Rule sheet, Calculated or Safety check, so she can tell a judgment from a sum.
  2. Confidence comes with its reasonA plain label and why it holds, never a bare score. When it’s low, there’s no Approve button.
  3. The rule cites its lawShe can check the work instead of taking it on trust.

Why this matters

Transparency is part of the product itself, not an explanation added after the fact.

07 · Decision 03

The agent can act. It can’t speak for you.

A wrong or rude letter lands on her, not on the AI. Anything that goes out in her name waits for her approval, permanently, however much she comes to trust it.

  1. Her identity is visibleThe letter goes out from her, in her name. Getbaq only drafts it.
  2. The consequence is visible₹3,499 and the reply-by date sit above the fold, before she approves.
  3. The evidence is visibleThe rule, its source and how sure the agent is, as backing.
  4. The irreversible action is isolatedOne strong button, unlike anything else on the screen, and six seconds to undo.

08 · Failure

The real product starts when the AI is wrong.

An agent has to know when it doesn’t know. Each failure is designed as a product state, not an error message, and each one ends in a next step, not a dead end.

Not sure

No rule fits well enough to trust.

No Approve button. It asks instead.

Missing

A fact she needs is absent.

One card asks for every gap at once.

Blocked

No grievance address it can trust.

Won’t guess. She gets the letter to send herself.

Out of scope

It isn’t a refund complaint.

Points her to e-Jagriti, a lawyer, or the bank.

Ignored

The company goes quiet.

The 1915 complaint is already prepared. Still her tap.

System failure

The AI can’t be reached.

A simpler reader or fixed template steps in, and says so.

09 · The case model

A conversation starts the case. The case carries it.

A conversation can answer a question. A case has to remember what happened and what happens next: the facts, the rule, the deadline, the status and the next action.

  1. The threadWhere she pastes, asks and reads. Finished steps fold into receipts.
  2. The case fileThe deadline, the facts, the rule and where the case is at. It doesn’t scroll away, and it outlives the conversation.
  3. The statusFifteen states behind the scenes; she sees four: needs your input, getting ready, waiting for their reply, resolved.

The difference

A chatbot ends when the conversation ends. Getbaq is designed to keep going after it.

11 · Working product, not a mock-up

I didn’t design a perfect AI in a static mock-up.

I built it and tested it against a real model, because the question that matters isn’t “what should the chatbot say?” but “what should the system do when something is wrong?”. Testing the rules, the dates and the letters turned up five mistakes that would have cost people money. The worst:

The rule sheet said

5 days

→

RBI says, for UPI

T+1

Every UPI user would have waited four days they didn’t have.

Response: UPI got its own rule, checked against the RBI circular, and every date is computed by code and labelled Calculated.

Two airline clocks

Risk: card and travel-site refunds treated as one clock. Response: separate rules, each sourced.

The timezone shift

Risk: near midnight, a deadline showed as missed. Response: one fixed timezone for every date.

The invented year

Risk: the model filled a missing year with a guess. Response: a year is kept only if she wrote one.

The wrong recipient

Risk: the UPI letter went to the shop, not her bank. Response: it asks for her bank and sends it there.

The strongest proof

None of these would have shown up in a static mock-up. Only one came from the model; the rest were in my own rules and code, which is exactly why they had to be tested, not assumed.

12 · Product decisions

Some ideas made it look smarter. They didn’t make it safer.

RejectedWhyChosen
Pure chatDecisions disappear into the conversation.Conversation, plus action cards, plus a persistent case
Making her sort the evidenceShe’d still be doing the organising.One box for anything; the agent structures it
An autonomy dialTrust shouldn’t decide who bears the consequence.Permission follows the consequence

The evidence decision took three versions. The first two made her sort her own input; the third made it the agent’s job.

13 · Status

What exists, and what isn’t proven yet.

Everything with one right answer is tested by code: 210 automated tests on dates, money, states and safety. The model’s judgment gets a scored evaluation instead. It’s built and ready, but not yet run:

8

legal rules, each read from the source and marked verified

30

real, sourced cases in the evaluation set

6

traps where the right answer is “no rule covers this”

3×

runs per case; it passes only if all three pass

Built

Working prototype. Gemini reads words and screenshots, finds the rule and drafts the letter, checked by code. Rule and evidence UI, approval with undo, every failure state, Google sign-in.

Simulated or planned

Sending, which is simulated. Sending from her own Gmail, and follow-ups while the app is closed.

Not yet proven

Model accuracy, as the 30-case evaluation hasn’t run. Real refunds recovered, real-user trust, and willingness to pay.

Looking back

Three things changed how I think about AI products.

  • 1

    Failure is product material. AI products need designed responses for uncertainty, conflict and failure, not just the happy path.

  • 2

    Model boundaries are interface decisions. Deciding what the AI, code and the human each control changes the product itself.

  • 3

    An agent earns its name by completing work. The goal isn’t a better answer. It’s moving her closer to resolution.

Cases, not conversations. Evidence, not prompts. Actions, not answers.

Getbaq isn’t shipped. The next proof is whether real people trust it with real money.
